# 3 Genetic Marker Amplification (PCR)

**Course day 3  
Wednesday, 23.09.2026**

**3.1** Everyone will receive two new small **PCR reaction tube** labelled with their previously assigned sample number and a PCR raction tube for the 5× PCR master mix (step 3.2). Place the reaction tubes in a rack.

**3.2** One person per lab group uses 10 µL and 100 µL/200 µL pipettes to **mix the 5× PCR master mix (Table 1; third column)** for one primer pair. Another person from your group will prepare the 5× PCR master mix for the other primer pair. Label the primer on the lit of the reaction tube to avoid confusion!

> [!IMPORTANT]
> We have two different mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) markers (Teleo02 and Vert 16S; see NOTE below). The pipetting scheme (Table 1) is the same for both primer pairs but is done seperatly!
>
> Only one 5× PCR master mix per primer pair (Teleo02 and Vert 16S) *and* per lab group (4 people) is needed. Each person will prepare their own two PCR reactions later (step 3.5).
>
> The group's 5× PCR master mix is prepared for five reactions to account for pipetting losses and ensure sufficient volume for all four PCR reactions per marker.

**3.3** Close the lit and vortex the PCR reaction tube with the 5× PCR master mix.

**3.4** Quick spin-down at maximum speed in the Gyro™ Plus Microcentrifuge.

**3.5** Everybody take the new PCR reaction tubes labelled with their previously assigned sample number and uses a 10 µL pipette to mix the final PCR reaction per primer pair each. **Mix 17 µL PCR master mix (containing Teleo02) with 1 µL DNA extract** and **mix 17 µL PCR master mix (containing Vert 16S) with 1 µL DNA extract (Table 1; second column)**. Label the primer on the lit of the reaction tube to avoid confusion!

***Table 1:** Components and volumes used for a single PCR reaction and for a master mix prepared for five reactions. The DNA extract ( * ) is obtained from the supernatant collected in step 2.11 and is added separately to each PCR reaction.
| Component                          | Volume per PCR reaction (step 3.5) | Volume for 5× PCR master mix (step 3.2) |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------: | --------------------------------------: |
| VWR® Taq DNA Polymerase Master Mix |                              10 µL |                                   50 µL |
| Forward (F) primer                 |                             0.4 µL |                                    2 µL |
| Reverse (R) primer                 |                             0.4 µL |                                    2 µL |
| H₂O (water)                        |                             6.2 µL |                                   31 µL |
| **Total PCR master mix**           |                          **17 µL** |                               **85 µL** |
| DNA extract*                       |                               1 µL |                                       — |
| **Final reaction volume**          |                          **18 µL** |                                       — |

> [!NOTE]
> In our course, we use two different primer pairs:
> - **Teleo02**: A widely used fish-specific primer pair often employed in environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding to detect, identify, and monitor freshwater and marine bony fish (teleost) biodiversity. It targets a hypervariable region of the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene, resulting in a roughly 170 base pairs (bp) long fragment amplified during PCR.
> - **Vert 16S**: A universal vertebrate primer pair often employed in eDNA metabarcoding. It targets a hypervariable region of the mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene, resulting in a roughly 250 bp long fragment amplified during PCR.
>
> Each primer pair comes with the forward primer (F) and the reverse primer (R) in seperate tubes, which you pipette together into the PCR reaction (step 3.2).

**3.6** Close the lit and vortex the PCR reaction tubes with the PCR reaction.

**3.7** Quick spin-down at maximum speed in the Gyro™ Plus Microcentrifuge.
