# 3 Genetic Marker Amplification (PCR)

**Course day 3  
Wednesday, 23.09.2026**

---
### Materials and Equipment
**Consumables:**

* Nitrile gloves
* PCR reaction tubes
* 10 µL pipette tips
* 100 µL or 200 µL pipette tips

**Reagents:**

* VWR® Taq DNA Polymerase Master Mix
* Teleo02 Forward (F) primer
* Teleo02 Reverse (R) primer 
* Vert 16S Forward (F) primer
* Vert 16S Reverse (R) primer
* MilliQ water (H₂O)

**Equipment:**

* Tube rack
* 10 µL pipette
* 100 µL or 200 µL pipette
* miniPCR® thermal cycler
* Gyro™ Plus Microcentrifuge
---

➤ **3.1** Everyone will receive two new small **PCR reaction tubes** labelled with their previously assigned sample number and each group a PCR reaction tube for the **6× PCR master mix** (step 3.2). Place the reaction tubes in a tube rack.

➤ **3.2** One person per lab group will use the 10 µL and 100 µL/200 µL pipettes to **prepare the 6× PCR master mix (Table 1, third column)** for one primer pair. Another person from the group will prepare the 6× PCR master mix for the other primer pair. Label the primer pair name on the lid of the reaction tube to avoid confusion!

> [!IMPORTANT]
> We have two different mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) markers (Teleo02 and Vert 16S; see NOTE below). The pipetting scheme (Table 1) is the same for both primer pairs but must be prepared separately.
>
> Only one **6× PCR master mix per primer pair (Teleo02 and Vert 16S) and per lab group (4 people)** is needed. Each person will prepare their own two PCR reactions later (step 3.5).

> ***Table 1:** Components and volumes used for a single PCR reaction and for a master mix prepared for six reactions. The DNA extract ( * ) is obtained from the supernatant collected in step 2.11 and is added separately to each PCR reaction. The group's 6× PCR master mix is prepared for six reactions to account for pipetting errors and ensure sufficient volume for all four PCR reactions per marker.*
>
> | Component                          | Volume per PCR reaction (step 3.5) | Volume for 6× PCR master mix (step 3.2) |
> | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------: | --------------------------------------: |
> | VWR® Taq DNA Polymerase Master Mix |                              10 µL |                                   60 µL |
> | Forward (F) primer                 |                             0.4 µL |                                  2.4 µL |
> | Reverse (R) primer                 |                             0.4 µL |                                  2.4 µL |
> | H₂O (MilliQ water)                 |                             6.2 µL |                                 37.2 µL |
> | **Total PCR master mix**           |                          **17 µL** |                              **102 µL** |
> | DNA extract*                       |                               1 µL |                                       — |
> | **Final reaction volume**          |                          **18 µL** |                                       — |

➤ **3.3** Close the lid and vortex the PCR reaction tube containing the 6× PCR master mix.

➤ **3.4** Perform a quick spin-down at maximum speed using the Gyro™ Plus Microcentrifuge (Figure 5).

➤ **3.5** Each person should take the new PCR reaction tubes labelled with their previously assigned sample number and prepare the final PCR reactions for each primer pair. Using a 100 µL/200 µL pipette as well as a 10 µL pipette, **combine 17 µL of the PCR master mix containing Teleo02 with 1 µL of DNA extract** and **17 µL of the PCR master mix containing Vert 16S with 1 µL of DNA extract (Table 1, second column)**. Label the primer pair on the lid of each reaction tube to avoid confusion!

> [!NOTE]
> In our course, we use two different primer pairs:
>
> * **Teleo02**: A widely used fish-specific primer pair often employed in environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding to detect, identify, and monitor freshwater and marine bony fish (teleost) biodiversity. It targets a hypervariable region of the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene and produces a PCR amplicon of approximately 170 base pairs (bp).
> * **Vert 16S**: A universal vertebrate primer pair often employed in eDNA metabarcoding. It targets a hypervariable region of the mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene and produces a PCR amplicon of approximately 250 bp.
>
> Each primer pair consists of a forward primer (F) and a reverse primer (R), which are provided in separate tubes. The primer pair is pipetted into the same PCR reaction (step 3.2).

➤ **3.6** Close the lid and vortex the PCR reaction tubes containing the final PCR reactions.

➤ **3.7** Perform a quick spin-down at maximum speed using the **Gyro™ Plus Microcentrifuge** (Figure 5).

➤ **3.8** Place the reaction tubes containing your PCR reactions into the **miniPCR® thermal cycler** (Figure 6). Wait for your fellow participants to place their samples in the thermal cycler as well.

>

➤ **3.9** Start the PCR programme (Table 2).

> ***Table 2:** PCR programme for both primer pairs.*
>
> | PCR Step                   | Temperature |  Time | Cycles |
> | -------------------------- | ----------: | ----: | -----: |
> | Initial denaturation       |       95 °C | 900 s |      1 |
> | Denaturation               |       95 °C |  30 s |     30 |
> | Annealing                  |       63 °C |  60 s |     30 |
> | Extension/Elongation       |       72 °C |  45 s |     30 |
> | Final extension/elongation |       72 °C | 600 s |      1 |
> | Hold                       |        4 °C |     ∞ |      — |

➤ **3.10** Perform a quick spin-down at maximum speed using the **Gyro™ Plus Microcentrifuge** (Figure 5).
